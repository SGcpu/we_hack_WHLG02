Key Legal Terms Glossary

Understand common legal terminology with our simplified definitions.

Defamation

Spreading false information that harms someone's reputation.

Defamation can be either:

  • Libel: Written false statements
  • Slander: Spoken false statements

To win a defamation case, you typically must prove the statement was false, published to others, and caused harm.

Due Process

Fair treatment under the law, ensuring all legal rights are respected.

Due process includes:

  • Right to a fair hearing
  • Notice of legal proceedings
  • Opportunity to present your side
  • Decision by an impartial judge

Both the 5th and 14th Amendments to the U.S. Constitution protect due process rights.

Liability

Legal responsibility for one's actions or omissions.

Common types of liability:

  • Civil Liability: Responsibility to compensate for damages
  • Criminal Liability: Responsibility for breaking criminal laws
  • Strict Liability: Responsibility regardless of fault or intent

Liability insurance exists to protect individuals and businesses from financial losses due to liability claims.

Statute of Limitations

Time limit for filing a lawsuit after an injury or violation.

These time limits vary by:

  • Type of case (personal injury, contract, etc.)
  • State or jurisdiction
  • When the harm was discovered

Once the statute of limitations expires, you generally lose your right to sue, with some exceptions.

Power of Attorney

Legal document giving someone authority to act on your behalf.

Common types include:

  • General: Broad powers over property and finances
  • Limited: Specific powers for defined situations
  • Medical: Healthcare decisions only
  • Durable: Remains valid if you become incapacitated

Creating a power of attorney requires proper documentation and often notarization.

Negligence

Failure to take proper care, resulting in damage or injury to another.

To prove negligence, you must establish:

  • Duty of care was owed
  • That duty was breached
  • The breach caused harm
  • Actual damages occurred

Negligence forms the basis for many personal injury lawsuits, including car accidents and medical malpractice.

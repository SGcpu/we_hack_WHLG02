Tenant Rights

Under the Rent Control Act and Model Tenancy Act, tenants in India have specific protections regarding rent increases, eviction procedures, and property maintenance. The Model Tenancy Act of 2021 caps security deposits at two months' rent for residential properties and limits annual rent increases.

Landlords must provide written notice (typically 3 months) before eviction and can only evict for specific reasons like property damage or rent default. All rental agreements should be in writing and registered with local authorities to be legally binding.

For disputes, tenants can approach Rent Control Tribunals or Rent Authorities established under state laws. Legal aid is available through District Legal Services Authority, and organizations like Housing Rights Association provide free consultations.

Quick Action Steps

  • Always insist on a written, registered rent agreement
  • Document the property condition before moving in
  • Keep all payment receipts and communication records
  • For disputes, file a complaint with local Rent Authority

Workplace Laws

The Industrial Disputes Act protects workers from unfair dismissal and provides mechanisms for resolving workplace conflicts. The Factories Act and Shops & Establishments Act regulate working hours (typically 48 hours weekly), overtime payments, and workplace safety standards.

Under the Maternity Benefit Act, women employees are entitled to 26 weeks of paid maternity leave. The Equal Remuneration Act mandates equal pay for equal work regardless of gender, while the Sexual Harassment of Women at Workplace Act requires all organizations to have Internal Complaints Committees.

Workers facing unfair treatment can file complaints with the Labor Commissioner's office, approach Industrial Tribunals, or contact specialized labor unions for support and guidance.

Filing a Workplace Complaint

  1. Document all instances of unfair treatment with dates and details
  2. Submit a written complaint to HR or management
  3. If unresolved, file a complaint with the Labor Commissioner
  4. For severe violations, approach the Industrial Tribunal in your region

Consumer Protection

The Consumer Protection Act (2019) significantly enhances consumer rights in India, covering product liability, unfair trade practices, and misleading advertisements. It establishes a three-tier dispute resolution system: District, State, and National Consumer Disputes Redressal Commissions.

Consumers have the right to safe products, accurate information, fair pricing, and effective grievance redressal. The Act also regulates e-commerce platforms, requiring transparent refund policies and disclosure of seller information. Penalties for misleading advertisements can reach ₹50 lakhs.

For defective products or services, consumers can file online complaints through the National Consumer Helpline or the INGRAM portal. Claims up to ₹1 crore can be filed at District Forums, with higher amounts directed to State and National Commissions.

Filing an Online Consumer Complaint

  1. Visit consumerhelpline.gov.in or the INGRAM portal
  2. Register with your contact details
  3. Submit your complaint with supporting documents
  4. Track your complaint using the provided reference number

Cybersecurity & Privacy

The Information Technology Act (2000) and its amendments form the foundation of India's cybersecurity laws, addressing hacking, data theft, identity fraud, and online privacy violations. The recent Digital Personal Data Protection Act (2023) enhances privacy protections, introducing consent requirements and data minimization principles.

Cybercrimes like unauthorized access (hacking), data theft, online harassment, identity theft, and phishing are punishable with imprisonment up to 7 years and fines up to ₹10 lakhs. Companies are legally required to implement reasonable security practices and procedures to protect sensitive personal data.

Victims of cybercrime can file complaints through the National Cyber Crime Reporting Portal (cybercrime.gov.in) or approach the local police's Cyber Cell. For data breaches, individuals can also lodge complaints with the Data Protection Board once fully established.

Reporting Cybercrime

  • For immediate assistance, call the Cyber Helpline: 1930
  • Report online through cybercrime.gov.in
  • Preserve all evidence (screenshots, emails, messages)
  • For financial fraud, report to your bank immediately

Criminal Law

The Indian Penal Code (IPC) defines criminal offenses and their punishments, while the Criminal Procedure Code (CrPC) outlines procedures for investigation, trial, and sentencing. The Evidence Act governs how evidence is collected and presented in court.

Criminal offenses are categorized as bailable (where getting bail is a right) or non-bailable (where bail is at the court's discretion). They're further classified as cognizable (police can arrest without warrant) or non-cognizable (requiring court permission for arrest).

Every accused person has the fundamental right to legal representation. If unable to afford a lawyer, free legal aid is provided through the Legal Services Authority. Other key rights include the right against self-incrimination, right to be informed of charges, and protection against arbitrary arrest.

Your Rights If Arrested

  • Right to know the grounds of arrest
  • Right to consult a lawyer of your choice
  • Right to be produced before a magistrate within 24 hours
  • Right to inform a relative/friend about the arrest
  • Right to free legal aid if unable to afford a lawyer

Human Rights

The Indian Constitution guarantees fundamental rights under Articles 12-35, including the right to equality, freedom, against exploitation, freedom of religion, cultural and educational rights, and constitutional remedies. These rights are enforceable through courts, with the Supreme Court as the ultimate guardian.

Specialized laws protect specific groups: SC/ST (Prevention of Atrocities) Act for Dalits and tribals, Protection of Women from Domestic Violence Act, Rights of Persons with Disabilities Act, and the Transgender Persons (Protection of Rights) Act.

The National Human Rights Commission (NHRC) investigates human rights violations, recommends compensation, and initiates proceedings against officials. Complaints can be filed online or via post, with no filing fee. State Human Rights Commissions function similarly at the state level.

Filing a Human Rights Complaint

  1. Visit nhrc.nic.in or use the HRC Net portal
  2. Submit your complaint with all relevant details and evidence
  3. Track your complaint using the provided reference number
  4. For urgent cases, call the NHRC helpline: 9810298900

Employment Law

Can I get fired without a warning?

156 8 12

I've been at my current job for 8 months. Yesterday, my manager called me into his office and informed me I was being terminated due to "performance issues." However, I've never received any formal warnings or negative performance reviews. Is this legal? What are my options?

Sarah Johnson, Esq.

Employment Law Specialist • 2 days ago

In most states, employment is considered "at-will," meaning employers can terminate employees for any reason (except illegal discrimination) without notice. However, if you have an employment contract or if company policy requires progressive discipline, you may have a case. I recommend checking your employee handbook and gathering documentation of your work performance. You could also file for unemployment benefits while you seek new employment.

Housing Law

Landlord entered without notice

243 12 18

I came home from work yesterday to find that my landlord had been in my apartment to "check the plumbing." He didn't give any advance notice. I feel like my privacy was violated. Is this legal? What rights do I have as a tenant?

Michael Chen, Esq.

Real Estate Attorney • 1 day ago

Most states require landlords to provide at least 24-48 hours notice before entering a tenant's unit, except in genuine emergencies (like an active water leak). If there was no emergency, your landlord likely violated your right to quiet enjoyment. I recommend: 1) Document the incident in writing, 2) Send a formal letter reminding your landlord of notice requirements, 3) Check your lease and local tenant laws for specific protections. If this is a recurring issue, you may have grounds for legal action.

Family Law

Child custody after relocation

187 6 9

I have primary custody of my children and need to relocate for a job opportunity that's about 200 miles away. My ex-spouse is threatening to take me to court to prevent this move. What do I need to know about relocating with children after divorce?

Rebecca Martinez, Esq.

Family Law Attorney • 3 days ago

Relocation cases can be complex. Courts typically consider whether the move is in the child's best interest. You'll need to demonstrate: 1) Legitimate reason for the move (better job, education, family support), 2) How you'll maintain the relationship between the children and the other parent, 3) How the move benefits the children. I recommend filing a petition for relocation before moving and proposing a modified visitation schedule. Each state has different notification requirements, so check your local laws. Consider mediation before court proceedings to reach an agreement.
